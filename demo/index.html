Essay

Reading Is the Feature

By Nadia Vance · 14 November · 11 minutes

A text editor is the only piece of software most programmers will still be using in twenty years, and the only one they open before they have decided what to do. That makes it less like a tool and more like a room, and nobody evaluates a room by its feature list. You notice, slowly, whether the light is good, whether the chair is the right height, whether you can hear yourself think in it.

Almost everything written about editors is written about editing: the modal keystroke, the macro, the language server that renames a symbol across four hundred files. Very little is written about the other ninety per cent of the work, which is reading. Reading your own code from last spring. Reading a stranger's patch at the end of a long day. Reading the log that explains why the build stopped at 03:12. The features that make reading easier are unglamorous, and nearly all of them are typographic.

The Case for a Narrow Column

The measure — the length of a line of text — is the oldest setting in the trade and the one an editor is least likely to let you change. A window maximised on a thirty-inch display gives a line of a hundred and eighty characters. The eye does not fail at the end of that line; it fails on the way back, hunting for the start of the next one, and the failure registers as fatigue rather than as an error. You do not think this is hard to read. You think I am tired, and you close the file.

Sixty-eight characters is the number this publication is set to, and it lives in exactly one place. Prose wants around sixty-five; code, with its indentation and its short identifiers, tolerates a little more. Neither wants a hundred and eighty.

A wide column does not make a page look generous. It makes the reader do the work the typesetter declined to do.

What the Rule Is Doing

A hairline is a promise that two things are different. It is the cheapest structural device available — one pixel, no elevation, no colour — and it survives every display density it will ever meet. Reach for a shadow instead and you have made a claim about physical depth that a page of text has no use for. The claim is expensive to render, it changes meaning between themes, and it says nothing the line was not already saying.

  • A rule between two rows says these are peers.
  • A rule under a heading says everything below belongs to it.
  • A rule with space on one side only says which side it belongs to.
  • A rule used four times on one screen says nothing at all.

In practice this is a single declaration, border-bottom: 1px solid var(--art-rule), plus the discipline never to write it twice on the same edge. The discipline is the hard part, and it is the part a design system can actually enforce.

Reading mode, in an editor, is usually shipped as a distraction-free toggle that hides the chrome and leaves the text at whatever width the window happens to be. That fixes the wrong thing. What a reader needs is the column narrowed, the leading opened, and the cursor turned into something that does not blink at them while they think.

;; Reading, not editing: sixty-eight columns and nothing else.
;; 阅读模式：一行六十八字，其余一概不管。
(defun compositor-reading-mode ()
  "Set the buffer for reading rather than for typing."
  (interactive)
  (setq-local fill-column   68
              line-spacing  0.35
              cursor-type   'bar)
  (visual-fill-column-mode 1)
  (variable-pitch-mode 1))

None of this is new. It is the same argument every printer's manual has made since the first one, restated for a medium where the page is a window and the reader can resize it to any width at all. The notes on measure accompanying this issue make it again, with better examples and worse jokes.

An editor that takes reading seriously does not need a new feature. It needs one number, held in one place, and an author willing to defend it. Everything else in this issue follows from that.

中文版 · The same argument, in Chinese

排版是一种照顾

版面从来不只是盛放文字的容器。它是一个关于读者愿意停留多久的判断：一行有多长、行与行之间留多少气口、标题用多重的字压住整页，这些都在替读者做决定。读者未必说得出哪里不对，却会在读到第三段时合上页面，然后以为是自己今天累了。

中文的行长和西文并不一样。西文按字母计，一行六十到七十五个字母最为稳妥；中文按方块字计，一行三十五到四十五个字就已经足够长。把西文的度量原封不动搬到中文正文上，行会长得让眼睛在折返时找不到下一行的开头。所以「一行多宽」这件事，必须按文字本身重新量一次，而不是把数字抄过来了事。

更容易出错的是字体。中西文混排时，若把中文字体放在字体栈的最前面，它会顺手接管所有拉丁字母——思源一类的字库本来就带着完整的西文字形，浏览器并不会替你分辨。正确的做法是把拉丁字体写在前面，中文字体紧随其后，只在遇到汉字时才交给它。顺序不是细节，顺序本身就是设计。
